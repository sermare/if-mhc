\noindent\textbf{The problem.} TCR-engineering therapies promise a transformative future for cancer
treatment, and with the 2024 approval of afamitresgene autoleucel that future has begun to
arrive\footnote{Singh, Mol.\ Ther.\ 32:3195, 2024.}. Yet cross-reactivity remains a fundamental barrier
to scaling them into personalized medicine. The danger is not hypothetical. An affinity-enhanced MAGE-A3
receptor that had cleared extensive preclinical testing caused fatal cardiac toxicity in two
patients\footnote{Linette et al., Cardiovascular toxicity and titin cross-reactivity of affinity-enhanced
T cells. Blood 122:863--871, 2013.}, cross-recognizing an unrelated self-peptide from the muscle protein
titin that was identified only after the fact\footnote{Cameron et al., A titin-derived HLA-A1 peptide as
cross-reactive target for MAGE-A3 T cells. Sci.\ Transl.\ Med.\ 5:197ra103, 2013.}.

\noindent\textbf{Why the field is stuck.} The bottleneck has never been imagination, it has been
throughput. Experimental validation is slow and costly, an untenable price when the presented peptide
space is astronomically large, on the order of $10^{11}$, and the fast-growing tumors these therapies
target leave only a narrow clinical window to act. The extreme polymorphism of HLA leaves binding data
sparse for most alleles and for underrepresented populations, precisely where personalized therapy is
needed most. Amino-acid scanning resolves one receptor at a time and does not scale to the combinatorics
of the peptide--TCR interaction.

\noindent\textbf{The approach.} The tools to test this at scale have only now come within reach. I would
bring two complementary tools of my own design, and pair them with inverse folding as the third
component. ESMCBA is a binding-affinity system built on protein language models with domain adaptation
that recovers usable signal even for data-scarce MHC alleles\footnote{Mares, Espinoza, Ioannidis,
Domain-specific pre-training of protein language models for pMHC-I binding. PMLR 311:304--325, 2025.}. A
structure-guided generative framework uses diffusion models conditioned on the MHC groove to sample
compatible peptides across high-priority alleles\footnote{Mares, Espinoza, Ioannidis, Structure-guided
pMHC-I libraries using diffusion models. ICML GenAI Biology Wksp, 2025.}. Inverse-folding models, which
read a fixed backbone and decode the sequences its geometry admits, supply what neither of the first two
can, namely which peptide positions a given receptor actually reads\footnote{Ribeiro-Filho et al.,
Structure-based deep learning for TCR design. PLOS Comput.\ Biol., 2024.}. Together they let me
computationally screen these interactions and chart the cross-reactive neighborhood of a receptor before
it ever reaches a patient.

\noindent\textbf{How it is proven.} Each prediction is falsifiable at the bench. The receptor either
engages the flagged peptide or it does not. Three experiments establish that, in order.

\noindent\textbf{Experiment 1, define the search axes.} Inverse-folding models will be applied to a panel
of solved pMHC--TCR structures twice, once with the receptor present and once with it removed. Positions
whose native recovery collapses on removal are the positions that receptor reads, and they define the
axes along which a cross-reactive peptide may differ. Molecular-dynamics ensembles of each complex,
rather than the single crystal snapshot, will set how much conformational variation still counts as the
same presentation, so the tolerance is measured rather than assumed. Predicted read positions will be
checked against measured mutations from SKEMPI and IEDB matched to each structure's own receptor by CDR3.
An architecture-matched model retrained without MHC or TCR structures serves as the control separating
learned interface chemistry from memorized training data.

\noindent\textbf{Experiment 2, retrospective recovery.} The full screen will be run blind on receptors
whose off-targets are already documented. MAGE-A3 is the primary test, since the titin epitope is known
and was missed by the methods in use at the time. A screen that fails to rank it fails outright, and this
is the pass condition for proceeding.

\noindent\textbf{Experiment 3, prospective validation.} Top-ranked candidates for a panel of characterized
receptors go to binding measurement, and those that bind go to functional assay. The primary deliverable
is a false-negative rate for cross-reactivity prediction on peptides never seen in training, a quantity
the field currently lacks. The loop closes within the appointment.

\noindent\textbf{What becomes possible.} A screen that runs in silico before infusion turns
cross-reactivity from a lethal surprise into a predictable and testable step, applied to every candidate
receptor rather than the few that fit an experimental budget, and making the next generation of cancer
therapies measurably safer. It generalizes to any therapy built on engineered molecular recognition,
where the failure mode is likewise specificity, not potency. The societal stake is measured in patients,
not benchmarks.

\noindent\textbf{Trajectory.} The first problem is chosen because it is winnable inside a year, the
second because it is worth a career. Near term the deliverable is the validated screen and its
false-negative rate. Beyond it lies whether specificity can be designed at the outset, not audited
after.

\noindent Recent unpublished work of my own indicates both routes are tractable on real pMHC--TCR
structures, which is what makes this worth building now.
